\subsection{TT-Cross}
\label{subsec:appendix-tt-cross}
% Keywords: tensor cross interpolation algorithm details, maxvol/rectangular
% maxvol, skeleton decomposition, pivoting strategy, convergence and error
% bounds, computational cost.

TT-cross builds a tensor train from evaluations of a black-box function, never
forming the full array~\cite{oseledets2010ttcross}. It is what makes
\cref{subsec:finding-qtt,subsec:tt-cross-geometry} possible.

\paragraph{The matrix case first.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item A rank-$r$ matrix $M$ is exactly reconstructed from $r$ rows $I$ and $r$
    columns $J$ by the \textbf{skeleton} (or CUR) formula
    \begin{equation}
      M \approx M(:,J)\, M(I,J)^{-1} M(I,:) .
      \label{eq:skeleton}
    \end{equation}
  \item The whole question is which rows and columns to pick. A badly
    conditioned $M(I,J)$ destroys the reconstruction.
  \item \textbf{maxvol} answers it: choose the $r \times r$ submatrix of maximum
    volume, i.e.\ largest $\abs{\det}$.\margintext{The maximum-volume principle
    is due to Goreinov and Tyrtyshnikov (2001); Oseledets and Tyrtyshnikov
    carried it up to tensor trains in 2010.} The resulting error is within a
    factor $(r+1)$ of the best rank-$r$ approximation in the Chebyshev norm.
  \item maxvol is computed greedily by row swaps, at cost $O(N r^2)$ per sweep.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{From matrices to tensor trains.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Apply~\eqref{eq:skeleton} recursively down the chain: at each bond,
    unfold the tensor into a matrix, choose pivots by maxvol, and keep the index
    sets.
  \item Sweeping left to right and back refines the index sets; the cost is
    $O(n \chi^3)$ function evaluations for $n$ tensors of bond dimension $\chi$
    --- \textbf{independent of $2^n$}.
  \item \textbf{Rectangular maxvol} keeps more than $r$ pivots, which improves
    robustness at modest extra cost, and is generally worth switching on.
  \item Rank adaptation: grow $\chi$ until the cross error --- measured on fresh
    sample points, not on the pivots --- stops decreasing.
\end{itemize}

\begin{remark}
  Convergence is heuristic. There is no guarantee for adversarial or noisy
  black boxes, and the failure modes listed in \cref{subsec:finding-qtt} are all
  observed in practice. Always validate on points the cross has never sampled.
\end{remark}

\subsection{DMRG}
\label{subsec:appendix-dmrg}
% Keywords: density matrix renormalization group, two-site sweep algorithm,
% local optimization/eigensolver, sweeping schedule, connection to
% variational MPS optimization, adaptive bond-dimension growth.

DMRG~\cite{white1992,schollwoeck2011} is the variational optimization of an MPS
by local updates. In these notes it appears as the linear solver of
\cref{subsec:linear-solvers}, as the pressure-Poisson step of
\cref{subsec:qtt-cfd-frameworks}, and as the all-at-once space--time solve of
\cref{subsec:dmd-space-time}.

\margintext{White introduced DMRG in 1992 to find ground states; its use as a
general-purpose linear solver on the MPS manifold is a later reading of the same
algorithm.}
\paragraph{The two-site sweep.}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Bring the MPS into mixed canonical form with the orthogonality centre at
    site $k$.
  \item Merge sites $k$ and $k+1$ into a single two-site tensor.
  \item Solve the \textbf{local problem} in that tensor alone --- an eigenproblem
    for ground states, a linear system for $Ax = b$ --- with every other tensor
    held fixed. The environments are contracted once and reused.
  \item Split the result by SVD, truncating to tolerance $\varepsilon$ or to a
    rank cap $\chi_{\max}$.
  \item Move the orthogonality centre one site along and repeat, sweeping right
    then left.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Practical notes.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Adaptive bond dimension} is the point of the two-site variant:
    the SVD in step~4 is free to return a larger rank than it started with.
  \item The local cost is up to $O(\chi^6)$ in the worst case; observed totals
    are much better (\cref{subsec:linear-solvers}).
  \item \textbf{Sweeping schedule.} Start with a loose tolerance and a low rank
    cap, and tighten both over successive sweeps. Two or three sweeps usually
    suffice from a warm start.
  \item \textbf{Warm starts} matter more than any other tuning parameter in a
    time-stepping context, because consecutive solutions are close.
  \item The one-site variant is cheaper but cannot grow the rank, which is what
    AMEn's residual enrichment repairs~\cite{dolgov2014amen}.
\end{itemize}
